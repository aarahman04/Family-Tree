# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

This project deals with people's family history — often the only surviving digital record of
someone's relatives, sometimes assembled after a death in the family. We hold ourselves to a
standard of care in the code; we hold the same standard in how we treat each other.

We pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics,
gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Focusing on what is best for the community and the project's users
- Showing empathy towards other community members — including toward people new to
  open source or new to genealogy software specifically

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- Harassment, insults, or derogatory comments, public or private
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission
- Trolling, or deliberately derailing discussion
- Any conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Data handling is a conduct issue here specifically

Because this project's whole purpose is handling real people's family records, one rule gets
called out explicitly rather than left implicit: **never post, attach, or paste a real
person's `.ftz`/`.ged` file, or any excerpt containing real names/dates/notes about real
people, anywhere in an issue, PR, discussion, or commit.** This applies even if it's your own
family, and even if you consider it already public. See `CONTRIBUTING.md` for how to report a
data-specific bug without doing this.

## Enforcement

Instances of unacceptable behavior may be reported to the project maintainer by opening a
private security advisory (see `SECURITY.md`) or by contacting the maintainer directly via
their GitHub profile. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits,
code, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct, and to
temporarily or permanently ban any contributor for behavior they deem inappropriate,
threatening, offensive, or harmful.
